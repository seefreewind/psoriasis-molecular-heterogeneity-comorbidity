We are pleased to submit our manuscript, "Molecular heterogeneity and multisystem shared genetic architecture define distinct biological layers of psoriasis", for consideration as an Article in Communications Biology.

Psoriasis is often studied through tissue transcriptomics or through germline susceptibility genetics, but these two maps are rarely tested against each other in the same analytical framework. In this study, we integrated cross-tissue psoriasis transcriptomics with independent bulk, single-cell, spatial and genetic evidence to ask whether reproducible tissue molecular heterogeneity corresponds to inherited multisystem comorbidity architecture.

The study makes three main contributions. First, a tested discrete k = 2 endotype representation did not meet the prespecified stability criterion, whereas continuous molecular programs were reproducible across cross-tissue modeling and independent paired-skin replication. Second, the retained tissue programs showed cellular and spatial contextual support but did not meet criteria for axis-specific genetic anchoring. Third, overall psoriasis susceptibility showed disease-specific shared genetic architecture, especially with coronary artery disease and near-neighbor psoriatic arthritis, with directionally heterogeneous local sharing for inflammatory bowel disease. Restricted regulatory prioritization and colocalization identified a limited set of candidate regulatory signals without direct gene-membership overlap with the retained molecular programs.

Together, these findings support a layered model of psoriasis biology. Reproducible tissue states and inherited multisystem liability are connected, but they are not interchangeable biological layers. We believe this distinction will be of interest to readers of Communications Biology because it addresses molecular stratification, immune-mediated disease genetics and the interpretation of multi-omics endotypes in a single disease setting, while preserving conservative boundaries around causality and clinical translation.
