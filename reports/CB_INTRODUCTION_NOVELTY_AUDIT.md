# Communications Biology Introduction Novelty Audit

Verification date: 2026-08-17.

## Purpose

This audit documents how the rewritten Introduction positions the manuscript against recent psoriasis molecular-heterogeneity and genetics literature. The manuscript does not claim novelty for the existence of transcriptomic heterogeneity in psoriasis. Its novelty is the explicit test of correspondence between tissue molecular heterogeneity and inherited multisystem comorbidity architecture.

## Major Recent Papers

### Rider et al., Communications Medicine, 2026

Paper: Rider et al. "Transcriptomic profiling and machine learning uncover gene signatures of psoriasis endotypes and disease severity." doi:10.1038/s43856-025-01325-4; PMID:41565778.

What it already established: The PSORT transcriptomic resource (E-MTAB-14509) contains rich skin and blood molecular structure. Rider et al. used WGCNA, ICA and machine-learning approaches to identify phenotype- and severity-associated gene modules, latent factors and predictive signatures across tissue compartments.

What it did not establish: It did not test the present manuscript's full decision chain: whether discrete patient classification is stable enough to carry endotype claims, whether continuous tissue programs are independently genetically anchored, and whether inherited multisystem comorbidity architecture maps onto those tissue programs.

How the current manuscript differs: The current manuscript uses the same PSORT transcriptomic resource as a substrate for a different question. It treats molecular heterogeneity as established context, tests discrete stability, freezes continuous programs, then separates tissue-program interpretation from overall psoriasis-comorbidity genetics.

Where cited in the Introduction: Paragraph 2, as the primary PSORT molecular-endotype context.

### Chen et al., Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology, 2026

Paper: Chen et al. "Uncovering a dual T helper 17/type 2 transcriptomic endotype in psoriasis." doi:10.1016/j.jaad.2026.06.131; PMID:42392458.

What it already established: A recent psoriasis transcriptomic study reported a dual Th17/type 2 endotype and strong IL-36 activation in Taiwanese psoriasis.

What it did not establish: It did not test whether transcriptomic endotype signals correspond to inherited systemic comorbidity architecture across CAD, PsA and IBD.

How the current manuscript differs: The current manuscript uses recent transcriptomic endotyping as field context, but focuses on reproducibility boundaries and the separation between tissue-state programs and germline shared disease architecture.

Where cited in the Introduction: Paragraph 2, as evidence that molecular endotyping is a current frontier.

### Shrotri et al., JID Innovations, 2025

Paper: Shrotri et al. "Transcriptomic Analysis Identifies Disease Severity and Therapeutic Response in Psoriasis." doi:10.1016/j.xjidi.2024.100333; PMID:39816445.

What it already established: Transcriptomic abnormalities can characterize psoriasis lesion severity and treatment-response patterns.

What it did not establish: It did not test whether stable tissue programs define inherited comorbidity architectures.

How the current manuscript differs: The present work uses molecular heterogeneity as a starting point and then asks whether it aligns with overall psoriasis shared genetics.

Where cited in the Introduction: Paragraph 2, as severity/treatment-associated transcriptomic heterogeneity.

### Ma et al., Nature Communications, 2023

Paper: Ma et al. "Single cell and spatial sequencing define processes by which keratinocytes and fibroblasts amplify inflammatory responses in psoriasis." doi:10.1038/s41467-023-39020-4; PMID:37308489.

What it already established: Single-cell and spatial sequencing can reveal keratinocyte-fibroblast inflammatory amplification in psoriatic tissue.

What it did not establish: It did not connect tissue-state heterogeneity to inherited multisystem comorbidity genetics.

How the current manuscript differs: The current manuscript uses single-cell and spatial evidence for directional contextualization of frozen bulk programs, not as a rescue route for mechanistic naming.

Where cited in the Introduction: Paragraphs 1 and 2, as tissue-state heterogeneity context.

### Castillo et al., Science Immunology, 2023

Paper: Castillo et al. "Spatial transcriptomics stratifies psoriatic disease severity by emergent cellular ecosystems." doi:10.1126/sciimmunol.abq7991; PMID:37267384.

What it already established: Spatial transcriptomics can stratify psoriatic disease severity and identify emergent cellular ecosystems, including tissue context beyond lesional inflammation alone.

What it did not establish: It did not test whether spatially organized tissue states correspond to inherited comorbidity architecture.

How the current manuscript differs: The current manuscript treats spatial organization as one tissue-state layer and then tests whether genetic comorbidity sharing constitutes the same or a separable layer.

Where cited in the Introduction: Paragraphs 1 and 2.

### Armstrong et al., Nature Reviews Disease Primers, 2025

Paper: Armstrong et al. "Psoriasis." doi:10.1038/s41572-025-00630-5; PMID:40571687.

What it already established: Psoriasis is a systemic, genetically influenced immune-mediated disease associated with psoriatic arthritis, cardiovascular disease, metabolic syndrome and psychiatric comorbidity.

What it did not establish: As a review, it does not test whether tissue molecular heterogeneity and inherited comorbidity architecture are the same biological layer.

How the current manuscript differs: The current manuscript operationalizes that systemic framing through genome-wide and local genetic correlation while preserving a separate tissue-state transcriptomic layer.

Where cited in the Introduction: Paragraph 1, as current systemic psoriasis context.

### Dand et al., Nature Communications, 2025

Paper: Dand et al. "GWAS meta-analysis of psoriasis identifies new susceptibility alleles impacting disease mechanisms and therapeutic targets." doi:10.1038/s41467-025-56719-8; PMID:40021644.

What it already established: A large psoriasis GWAS meta-analysis of 36,466 cases and 458,078 controls identified 109 distinct psoriasis susceptibility loci, connected susceptibility variants to immune regulation, and assessed genome-wide correlation with 345 disease and health-related traits.

What it did not establish: It treated psoriasis susceptibility at the disease level and did not partition shared systemic genetic architecture by tissue molecular programs.

How the current manuscript differs: The current manuscript uses overall psoriasis susceptibility for comorbidity genetics only after testing and rejecting axis-specific genetic anchoring.

Where cited in the Introduction: Paragraphs 1 and 3.

### Patrick et al., Nature Communications, 2022

Paper: Patrick et al. "Shared genetic risk factors and causal association between psoriasis and coronary artery disease." doi:10.1038/s41467-022-34323-4; PMID:36323703.

What it already established: Psoriasis and coronary artery disease share genetic risk factors, supporting cardiovascular shared-architecture relevance.

What it did not establish: It did not ask whether psoriasis-CAD genetic sharing is mediated by specific psoriasis tissue molecular programs.

How the current manuscript differs: The current manuscript places CAD sharing alongside PsA and IBD and tests whether shared genetic architecture overlaps frozen tissue programs or remains a separable layer.

Where cited in the Introduction: Paragraphs 1 and 3.

### Stuart et al., AJHG, 2015; Ellinghaus et al., AJHG, 2012; Li et al., ARD, 2013

Papers: Stuart et al. doi:10.1016/j.ajhg.2015.10.019; Ellinghaus et al. doi:10.1016/j.ajhg.2012.02.020; Li et al. doi:10.1136/annrheumdis-2012-202143.

What they already established: PsA, cutaneous psoriasis and Crohn disease have clinically and genetically informative relationships.

What they did not establish: These studies did not test whether psoriasis tissue molecular programs define separable inherited comorbidity architectures.

How the current manuscript differs: The present manuscript uses these established comorbidity links as representative systemic genetics context, then tests genome-wide and local architecture under a frozen overall-psoriasis exposure.

Where cited in the Introduction: Paragraph 3.

## Final Novelty Position

The rewritten Introduction defends the manuscript as a layered biology study, not as another psoriasis endotype discovery paper. The novelty is:

1. testing discrete molecular classification before accepting endotype language;
2. retaining reproducible continuous tissue programs while restricting their claim strength;
3. testing whether those programs are genetically anchored before entering comorbidity genetics;
4. showing that overall inherited psoriasis comorbidity architecture is better handled as a separable genetic layer.
